## WEIGHT RECALIBRATIONS

### Recalibration 1: H-Tag Elevation Protocol

**Failure:** H-tagged horses won 3 of 6 races but were placed as Top Strike in zero of those races. The model identifies hidden potential accurately but systematically underweights it in the selection hierarchy.

**Adjustment:** When an H-tagged horse is identified in the analysis, apply a +1 elevation modifier to its selection ranking. If an H-tag is assigned alongside a P-tag or S-tag Top Strike, the H-tagged horse should be considered for co-Top Strike status or promoted to Top Strike when the primary selection has identifiable vulnerabilities.

### Recalibration 2: Front-Runner on Heavy Ground Weighting

**Failure:** In Race 3, Swindon Village was correctly identified as the likely pace-setter but was assigned a "stamina query" and placed as Danger. He won making all.

**Adjustment:** On Heavy ground (GoingStick sub-4.0), confirmed front-runners with proven soft/heavy ground form should receive a +15% probability uplift. The logic: on truly testing ground, the energy cost of making up ground is exponentially higher than on good ground. Front-runners who can sustain are at a structural advantage because closers cannot accelerate through deep ground.

### Recalibration 3: Market Anomaly Signal Inversion

**Failure:** In Race 2, Lancelot Allen's short price was flagged as an "anomaly" (implying it was wrong) rather than a signal of stable confidence. He won.

**Adjustment:** In maiden/novice races, when a lightly-raced horse is shorter in the market than their public form suggests, this should be treated as a POSITIVE signal (stable confidence, private information) rather than a negative anomaly. The market anomaly flag should be split into two categories: (a) Anomaly-Overbet (horse shorter than form warrants, likely public bias) and (b) Anomaly-Informed (horse shorter than form warrants in a context where private information is likely, such as maidens/novices).

### Recalibration 4: NR Contingency Protocol

**Failure:** Two Top Strike selections (Talakan R2, potentially Harmony Heights R6) were non-runners. The model had no protocol for re-evaluating the race framework post-withdrawal.

**Adjustment:** Implement a NR Contingency Protocol: when a Top Strike or Best Bet is withdrawn, the entire race framework must be re-assessed. The remaining selections should not simply be promoted — the race dynamics have changed and may favour different horses entirely. Specifically, when a clear favourite is withdrawn, H-tagged horses in the field should be elevated as the primary beneficiaries of the resulting open race.

### Recalibration 5: S-Tag Reliability Ceiling

**Failure:** S-tagged (Solid) horses went 3/5 validated but the 2 failures included a winner wrongly tagged as "solid with a stamina query" (Swindon Village) and a 6th-place finisher tagged as "solid and reliable" (Havaila).

**Adjustment:** The S-tag is too broad. Split into S-PROVEN (demonstrated at this level/conditions) and S-ASSUMED (solid profile but untested in the specific conditions of this race). Swindon Village should have been S-PROVEN (won making all on soft ground). Havaila should have been S-ASSUMED (C&D form but not in this specific competitive context).

### Recalibration 6: P-Tag Overconfidence Correction

**Failure:** P-tagged (Proven/Potential) horses went 1/4 validated. Bobby's Nelson (P, 3rd), Robber's Bridge (P, PU), Katie's Melody (P, last) all failed. Only Major Fortune (P, 2nd) validated.

**Adjustment:** The P-tag is being applied to two different concepts: "Proven form" and "Potential for improvement." These should be separated. P-PROVEN should apply to horses with demonstrated form at the required level. P-PROGRESSIVE should apply to horses on an upward trajectory. The failures were all P-PROVEN horses who were assumed to repeat — the tag should carry a recency decay factor.

French racing surfaces, even when described as "heavy," may not be equivalent to UK Heavy at Newbury in February. This is a data quality issue — the model should discount cross-jurisdiction ground comparisons.

Giantsgrave had the highest Topspeed (119) in Race 2 but fell. Speed figures from non-heavy ground should be discounted by 20-30% when applied to Heavy ground races.

1. **GoingStick sub-3.0 (Hurdle course) = Attrition conditions.** Expect 30-40% non-completion rates in large-field handicaps.

2. **Front-runners with proven soft/heavy form are the strongest play.** The closing speed required to overhaul a leader on Heavy ground at Newbury is rarely available.

3. **Point-to-point form is the gold standard for stamina verification** on Heavy ground over 2m4f+. It should be weighted above hurdle/chase form on "soft" ground.

4. **First-time headgear on Heavy ground** — Personal Ambition won in first-time blinkers (Race 1). The combination of a new stimulus and testing conditions can produce dramatic form reversals. This is a viable angle when the horse has previously shown ability.
