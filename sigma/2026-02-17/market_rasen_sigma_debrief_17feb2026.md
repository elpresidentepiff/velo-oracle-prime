## WEIGHT RECALIBRATIONS

### Recalibration 1: Favourite Tax Doctrine Over-Application (Race 1)
**Failure:** Senator was tagged as a Favourite Tax candidate and opposed as Top Strike. He won at BSP 2.13.
**Root Cause:** The Favourite Tax doctrine was applied to a horse with a genuinely superior RPR (118) in a weak field. The doctrine is designed to identify false favourites, not to oppose every favourite.
**Adjustment:** Favourite Tax doctrine must include a **quality gate**: if the favourite's RPR exceeds the second-best by 5+ points AND the field is weak (Class 4+ with no proven Group/Listed form), the Tax is unlikely to apply. The favourite is the favourite for a reason.

### Recalibration 2: Exposed Tag in Non-Runner Scenarios (Race 5)
**Failure:** Aire Spray was tagged "Exposed" and dismissed. She won after two non-runners reshaped the race.
**Root Cause:** The model does not have a protocol for re-evaluating tags when non-runners are confirmed. The original assessment was made against an 11-runner field; the actual race had 9 runners with a different competitive dynamic.
**Adjustment:** Implement a **Non-Runner Recalibration Protocol**: when non-runners remove 2+ runners or remove the Top Strike, all remaining runners must be re-evaluated. Tags assigned in the original assessment are provisional until the final field is confirmed.

### Recalibration 3: Sleeper Tag Precision (Races 1, 5, 6, 7)
**Failure:** S-tagged horses had a 43% validation rate — the lowest of any tag.
**Root Cause:** The Sleeper tag is too broad. It encompasses genuine dark horses (Scottish King with top Topspeed, On Jack with strong connections) and wishful thinking (Knighton at 25/1, Le Breton in a bumper).
**Adjustment:** Split the S tag into two sub-categories: **S1 (Data Sleeper)** — supported by at least one top-3 metric (Topspeed, RPR, OR differential); **S2 (Speculative Sleeper)** — based on pedigree, connections, or intangibles only. S1 tags should be weighted for selection consideration; S2 tags should be logged for tracking only.

### Recalibration 4: Bumper Analysis Model (Race 7)
**Failure:** Jessmarvel was backed on proven form; On Jack won as a debutant.
**Root Cause:** The bumper environment is structurally different from hurdle/chase racing. Proven form is a weaker signal because the sample sizes are tiny and debutants with strong connections can outperform on raw ability.
**Adjustment:** In bumper races, **market support for debutants must be weighted as a primary signal**, not secondary. If a debutant shortens to 3/1 or less in a bumper, they must be included in the framework as a minimum Danger pick, regardless of the absence of form data.

### Recalibration 5: Railway Bell Layoff Thesis (Race 4)
**Failure:** Railway Bell was tagged H (Hidden weakness — layoff) but finished 2nd.
**Root Cause:** The 410-day layoff was treated as a binary negative. In reality, the horse was from a top stable (Fergal O'Brien) with a strong jockey booking (Jonathan Burke), and the layoff may have been planned to target a specific race.
**Adjustment:** Layoff tags must be **modulated by stable quality**. Top-10 trainers returning horses after long absences are often doing so with intent. The H tag for layoff should only apply to horses from lower-tier operations where the absence is more likely injury-related.
