## Mechanism Extracts

### 1. Rejected / Difficult Horses Can Hide Elite Ability

Observation: Bates Motel was unwanted by another major trainer, backward, weak, aggressive, and difficult, yet became champion older horse.

Inference: Rejection by a big yard is not always negative. Some horses are rejected because they are hard to manage, not because they lack ability. Trainer patience and handling can unlock hidden class.

Candidate Tags: `REJECTED_HORSE_EDGE`, `TEMPERAMENT_UNLOCK`, `NON_OBVIOUS_CLASS`

### 2. Movement Can Deceive: Walk/Trot Versus Gallop

Observation: Royal Heroine had poor walk/trot action but exceptional gallop stretch and broke records.

Inference: Visual paddock/action negatives may be misleading if the horse transforms at gallop. Some physical defects are only false negatives for racing performance.

Candidate Tags: `BAD_WALK_GOOD_GALLOP`, `ACTION_FALSE_NEGATIVE`

### 3. Specific Jockey-Horse Pairing Can Be Essential

Observation: Shantou was difficult and "only" Frankie could get him to go. Benny The Dip needed a late jockey solution. Raven's Pass required an America-capable rider for the Breeders' Cup.

Inference: The correct jockey is not generic. Some horses require a specific feel, tactical culture, or racecourse experience. A jockey booking can be a functional key, not just reputation.

Candidate Tags: `JOCKEY_FIT_KEY`, `COURSE_EXPERIENCE_BOOKING`, `HORSEMANSHIP_OVER_PROFILE`

### 4. Race Placement Is A Campaign Weapon

Observation: Oasis Dream was campaigned as a sprinter despite pedigree pressure to run over a mile. Stradivarius was placed into the Goodwood Cup because three-year-olds received weight. Lord North was spaced for a repeat Dubai target.

Inference: Elite trainers use race conditions, allowances, trip shape, and timing to weaponize placement. The best target is often not the obvious public/pedigree target.

Candidate Tags: `PLACEMENT_EDGE`, `ALLOWANCE_EXPLOIT`, `TRIP_REALITY_OVER_PEDIGREE`, `TARGET_SPECIALIST`

### 5. Trainer-Jockey Track Walks Are Tactical Intelligence

Observation: Golden Horn's Arc ride was planned by walking the track and identifying an outside line, delayed crossover, and breather point. Enable's Chantilly Arc plan centered on escaping the draw in the first 400m.

Inference: For major races, the winning tactic can be created before the race: draw geometry, camber, rail movement, pace position, breather point, and when to commit.

Candidate Tags: `TRACK_WALK_TACTICS`, `DRAW_ESCAPE_PLAN`, `BREATHER_POINT`, `COMMITMENT_POINT`

### 6. Draw Can Beat Ability

Observation: Tagruda and Enable examples show Arc draw can be cruel. A bad draw forces three bad options and can create unfair blame for jockeys.

Inference: Draw/track configuration must be treated as a tactical constraint, not just a statistical variable. A brilliant horse can be compromised before the race starts.

Candidate Tags: `DRAW_CRUELTY`, `TRACK_GEOMETRY_RISK`, `JOCKEY_BLAME_FALSE_POSITIVE`

### 7. Opposition Tactics Are Legitimate Weapons

Observation: Gosden described Ballydoyle/other runners attempting to hold a rival in, pace dynamics disappearing when horses were withdrawn, and Hunting Horn affecting Enable/Magical race shape.

Inference: Pacemakers, blockers, pressure horses, and pace withdrawals are strategic race weapons. Market may not reflect tactical interference risk.

Candidate Tags: `TACTICAL_BLOCKER`, `PACE_WEAPON`, `STABLEMATE_TACTICS`, `RIVAL_HOLD_DOWN`

### 8. Ground / Heat / Surface Can Reverse Class

Observation: Golden Horn disliked soft/loose Breeders' Cup surface; Cracksman disliked heat/quick ground but thrived on soft; Roaring Lion hated slop; Raven's Pass wanted faster ground.

Inference: Going is not one-dimensional. Heat, looseness, kickback, downhill tracks, and surface composition can matter as much as official going.

Candidate Tags: `GROUND_SPECIFIC_CLASS`, `HEAT_KNOCK_RISK`, `SURFACE_TEXTURE_RISK`, `DOWNHILL_TRACK_DISLIKE`

### 9. Blinkers Are Not Always A Negative

Observation: Gosden described blinkers as a focusing/sharpening tool, not only a sign of non-trying or desperation.

Inference: First-time or re-applied headgear needs context: focus, sharpness, temperament, or trainer philosophy. Blanket negative interpretation is poor.

Candidate Tags: `BLINKER_FOCUS_TOOL`, `HEADGEAR_CONTEXT_REQUIRED`

### 10. A Trainer May Know When The Public Target Is Wrong

Observation: Golden Horn's owner doubted Derby stamina based on pedigree; Gosden accepted the concern but used Dante evidence to justify supplementing. Oasis Dream's pedigree suggested a mile but sprinting was correct.

Inference: Pedigree narratives can mislead the public and even owners. Work, race evidence, and trainer feel can override paper breeding.

Candidate Tags: `PEDIGREE_NARRATIVE_TRAP`, `TRAINER_FEEL_OVERRIDE`, `EVIDENCE_OVER_BOOK`

### 11. Unbeaten Records Can Create Cowardice

Observation: Gosden said if you are afraid of defeat, you stop being brave and stop letting horses express themselves.

Inference: A trainer willing to risk defeat may place a horse more ambitiously. Public overvalues unbeaten records; professionals may value expression, target logic, and long-term campaign.

Candidate Tags: `UNBEATEN_RECORD_TRAP`, `BRAVE_CAMPAIGNING`, `LOSS_NOT_FATAL`

### 12. Handicap Plotting Exists And Can Be Deliberate

Observation: Halling's Cambridgeshire path was described as a deliberate plan after injury, including a poor prep run, weight timing, and a needed win before weights closed.

Inference: Handicap plots are real. They can involve controlled exposure, timing around weights, and trainer knowledge that a horse is materially better than its mark.

Candidate Tags: `HANDICAP_PLOT`, `WEIGHT_CLOSURE_TIMING`, `CONTROLLED_EXPOSURE`, `MARK_SUPPRESSION`

### 13. Owner-Breeder Policies Shape Campaigns

Observation: Sheikh Hamdan's family policy retired fillies at three, later regretted with Tagruda. Prince Khalid kept Enable in training partly for emotional/racing joy.

Inference: Owner philosophy affects whether a horse is campaigned for sport, breeding, cashflow, stallion value, or legacy. This can explain otherwise non-obvious campaign choices.

Candidate Tags: `OWNER_POLICY_SIGNAL`, `BREEDING_PRIORITY`, `LEGACY_CAMPAIGN`, `SPORTING_OWNER`

### 14. Gelding Can Unlock Campaign Longevity

Observation: Lord North was difficult/panicky and was gelded; trainer notes trainers like gelding because they can race them longer.

Inference: Gelding can change the entire campaign economy: no stallion pressure, more spacing, longer career, targeted international races.

Candidate Tags: `GELDING_UNLOCK`, `NO_STALLION_PRESSURE`, `LONG_CAMPAIGN_TARGETING`

### 15. Yard Knowledge Of Families Reduces Mistakes

Observation: Gosden emphasized knowing families: mother, father, siblings, uncle, aunt; it helps avoid repeating mistakes.

Inference: Trainer history with bloodlines is an intent edge. A yard that knows the family may place or manage a horse better than public data implies.

Candidate Tags: `FAMILY_KNOWLEDGE_EDGE`, `BLOODLINE_HANDLING_MEMORY`
