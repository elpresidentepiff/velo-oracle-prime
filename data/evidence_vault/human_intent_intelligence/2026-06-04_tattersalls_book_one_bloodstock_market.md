## Mechanism Extracts

### 1. Sales Value Is Manufactured Before Racing

Observation: Breeders and consignors spend weeks conditioning, walking, polishing, branding, and positioning yearlings so buyers can imagine the finished racehorse.

Inference: Auction value is not just pedigree. Presentation, muscle definition, walk, brand, timing, and buyer imagination are all manufactured signals.

Candidate Tags: `SALES_PREP_SIGNAL`, `PRESENTATION_ALPHA`, `VALUE_MANUFACTURE`

### 2. Public Auction Is Theatre And Pressure

Observation: Auctioneers manage atmosphere, bid increments, bidder visibility, and momentum. Some bidders hide so rivals do not see their interest.

Inference: The ring itself is a pressure machine. Price can reflect theatre, rivalry, ego, and scarcity, not just expected racing ability.

Candidate Tags: `AUCTION_THEATRE`, `BIDDER_VISIBILITY_GAME`, `EGO_CAPITAL_PRESSURE`

### 3. Foreign Capital Distorts Valuation Bands

Observation: Foreign money accounts for a major share of revenue; Hong Kong, Japan, Middle East, China, and Qatar buyers bring stronger buying power than many domestic owners.

Inference: Sale price can be a capital-flow signal rather than a pure horse-quality signal. Domestic value buyers may operate in a different market tier.

Candidate Tags: `FOREIGN_CAPITAL_PRESSURE`, `DOMESTIC_VALUE_DISCOUNT`, `EXPORT_RESALE_PATH`

### 4. Reserve Price Is A Hidden Intent Signal

Observation: Owners set reserve prices to protect horses. A Kitten's Joy colt failed at 130k because the owner wanted 200k. The filly's reserve was lowered from 400k to 350k but sold for 700k.

Inference: Reserve movement reveals vendor confidence, risk tolerance, and willingness to let go. A buyback is not necessarily lack of interest; it may be protection.

Candidate Tags: `RESERVE_INTENT`, `VENDOR_PROTECTION`, `BUYBACK_NOT_NEGATIVE`

### 5. Vetting / X-Ray Risk Can Change Market Interest Late

Observation: Yearlings require x-rays. A joint anomaly on a promising horse became a question of interpretation despite no lameness.

Inference: Vet findings can suppress or redirect buyer interest, sometimes over small developmental issues. Market weakness may reflect vet caution rather than athletic inferiority.

Candidate Tags: `XRAY_MARKET_SUPPRESSION`, `VET_INTERPRETATION_RISK`, `DEVELOPMENTAL_ANOMALY`

### 6. Buyer Work Happens Before The Ring

Observation: Agents inspect 140-150 horses per day, balance budget/conformation/pedigree, vet shortlists, and must be ready for late foreign orders.

Inference: The visible bid is the end of a private filtering pipeline. If a horse is on a second-look list or vetted multiple times, that is meaningful pre-ring demand.

Candidate Tags: `SECOND_LOOK_SIGNAL`, `VETTED_BY_BUYER`, `PRIVATE_SHORTLIST_DEMAND`

### 7. One-In-Ten Reality Versus Auction Dream

Observation: Even with strong bloodlines, only about one in ten progeny becomes a successful racehorse; yet prices can reach millions because buyers are purchasing the dream.

Inference: Pedigree price embeds lottery premium. High price is a dream/option value, not proven ability.

Candidate Tags: `DREAM_PREMIUM`, `PEDIGREE_LOTTERY`, `PRICE_NOT_ABILITY`

### 8. Physical Type Must Match Intended Job

Observation: Agents expect sprinters, milers, and stayers to look different. They balance conformation, pedigree, and budget.

Inference: A yearling's physique should be evaluated against expected future race type, not generic beauty. Wrong body-for-job creates future misplacement risk.

Candidate Tags: `TYPE_MATCHING`, `BODY_FOR_JOB`, `SPRINTER_MILER_STAYER_PHYSICAL`

### 9. Broodmare Value Can Outweigh Race Value

Inference: Female purchase price may reflect residual breeding value more than expected race performance. This affects owner incentives and campaign conservatism later.
